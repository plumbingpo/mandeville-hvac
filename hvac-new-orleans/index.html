When Your System Fails in New Orleans

Heating and cooling failures rarely wait for a convenient day, which is why we offer prompt, flexible scheduling for most situations. When you call, you'll reach a real person who gets a technician headed to your door without delay. Arrival in the area is typically within a day for urgent calls, and a dead AC in a heat wave or a furnace out on a freezing night is exactly the kind of call we move to the front of the line.

Here's what we see most often when something breaks at home: warm air from the AC, a condenser that hums but won't start, a furnace that ignites and shuts off, a breaker that keeps tripping, an evaporator coil that has iced over, a condensate drain backed up into the furnace cabinet, or a thermostat that has gone blank. Most of them come down to parts already on our trucks: capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and standard fittings. You'll see the price up front, and that's the price you pay.

Two common situations call for a quick step from you before our technician gets there. The first is an iced-over air conditioner: turn the system off at the thermostat and set the fan to "on" so warm indoor air thaws the coil, because continuing to run it can overflow the drain pan and damage the compressor. The second is a furnace that keeps cycling on and off or gives off a burning smell. Turn it off at the service switch and call us. That small step can prevent a much larger repair.

Keeping Your New Orleans HVAC System Running Longer

Many mid-season breakdowns trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Change your filter every one to three months, keep plants and shrubs at least two feet from the outdoor condenser, and rinse the condenser coil every spring. Then book a professional tune-up twice a year, one for cooling and one for heating, so our technicians can catch small problems while they're still cheap to fix.

Age matters too. Most central air conditioners run 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15 with regular service. If your system is past that window and showing symptoms, such as rising energy bills, rooms that never quite reach temperature, or a service call every season, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and far less stressful than an emergency one in the middle of a cold snap.

Minor issues don't stay minor when they're ignored. Take a weak capacitor: replacing it is a quick, inexpensive repair, but left alone for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and just like that a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a miserable night.
