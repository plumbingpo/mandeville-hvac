When Your System Fails in Lafayette

If your system quits, how fast help shows up matters more than anything else. That's why we keep our schedule flexible across the area and answer every call with a live person. Urgent calls usually get a technician within a day, and we prioritize no-heat and no-cool calls ahead of routine work.

These are the problems that come up most when something breaks at home: an air conditioner blowing warm air, a condenser that hums but won't start, a furnace that lights for a few seconds and quits, a breaker that trips every time the system kicks on, an evaporator coil that has iced over, a condensate drain backed up into the furnace cabinet, or a thermostat that has gone blank. Nearly all of these trace back to parts already on our trucks: capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and standard fittings. You'll see the price up front, and that's the price you pay.

Two situations call for quick action from you before our technician gets there. The first is an iced-over air conditioner: turn the system off at the thermostat and set the fan to "on" so warm indoor air thaws the coil, because continuing to run it can overflow the drain pan and hurt the compressor. The second is a furnace that short-cycles or gives off a burning smell. Turn it off at the service switch and give us a call. Those few minutes can save you from a much larger repair.

Keeping Your Lafayette HVAC System Running Longer

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep shrubs and grass trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and have a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still inexpensive.

Age matters too. Most central air conditioners last 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15 with regular service. If your system is past that window and showing symptoms, such as climbing utility bills, rooms that never quite reach temperature, or a service call every season, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and far less stressful than an emergency one during the first heat wave of summer.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Consider a weak capacitor: replacing it is a short, affordable repair, but left alone for a season, it can burn out the compressor, and just like that a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and spares you a miserable night.
